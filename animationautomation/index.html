    The Pipeline

    Your agent does ninety percent
    of the production.

    Not the prompting. Not one step. The production. From the reference sheets that hold your character together, to the script, to every clip, to the voice, the music, the edit, the captions — and out the door to the platforms. Read that list again slowly. That is the whole job.

    01
    It builds the cast
    Character & scene reference sheets

    Your agent builds the character sheet — five angles, one face — and the scene sheets that lock the world she lives in. Every asset gets logged and reused, so shot forty still looks like shot one. This is where consistency stops being luck and becomes a filing system.

    02
    It writes the film
    Idea scoring · script · storyboard

    It scores your idea out of twenty and tells you honestly when it's a twelve — then offers three better ones. It writes the beats, cuts every line to fifteen words, and builds the locked-world table that stops the model redecorating your kitchen between shots.

    03
    It shoots it
    Your pick of three image models · Seedance 2.0

    Stills first — cheap to iterate, and you choose the image model every production: Nano Banana 2 (cheapest, fastest), GPT Image, or Seedream 5.0 Pro (strongest at following instructions). Switch mid-reel if one blocks a prompt — you are always the final judge. Then each approved still becomes the first frame of its clip, generated on Seedance 2.0 — the newest and most powerful video model available today. Native audio, real performance, and the fidelity that makes people forget to ask whether it's AI. That first-frame anchor is precisely what stops your character morphing mid-reel. It fires the generations in parallel, polls them, retries the failures, and files everything where it belongs.

    04
    It gives her a voice
    Cloned voice · Suno score

    A cloned voice — hers, or yours — carrying the accent and the cadence, because get the voice wrong and the comments will roast you. Then an original Suno track scored to the mood of the cut. No library, no licensing, no copyright headache.

    05
    It cuts it
    Assembly · captions · export

    Crossfades, audio blends, burned-in captions positioned above the Instagram UI zone, exported to spec. You never open an editor. If the caption timing is off, you say so, and it re-renders in seconds.

    06
    It ships it
    Posting · AI label · performance log

    Out to the platforms, AI-labelled — which is required, and also the best content angle you have. Forty-eight hours later it logs the views, the shares and the comments people actually quoted. Those quotes are next week's content.

    So what's your ten percent? Taste.

    Deciding who she is. Knowing which take is the one. Saying no at the gates. The machine will happily produce a hundred beautiful, forgettable reels — judgement is the part it can't do for you, and it's the part this class actually teaches.

    Automation without a handbrake is just a faster way to make rubbish. Nothing expensive gets generated until you've said yes.

    Gate One
    The script

    Not a frame is generated until you approve the storyboard. Ideas are free. Video is not.

    Gate Two
    The stills

    Every frame checked before it moves: does the face match the sheet? The room? Is the prop the right size?

    Gate Three
    The performance

    Face, scene, realism, acting. All four, or it goes back. You don't ship "close enough" at 1am.

    Questions

    Answered.

    Do I need any video, design or coding experience?

    None. The whole point of an automated pipeline is that you describe what you want and the tools do the labour. If you can write a clear sentence, you can direct a scene.

    Is this the same as your AI Animation Masterclass?

    No — this is v2.0, and it's a different course. The original teaches you to make a video by hand with Google Flow, Suno and CapCut. This one drops the manual tools entirely and teaches you to build a character and run an automated production pipeline with your agent and Higgsfield. If you've done the first one, this is the sequel. If you haven't, you don't need it.

    How much is it, and how do I book?

    The masterclass is S$240 per seat — three and a half hours face-to-face with Dr. Andrew Chew, hands-on, with follow-up query support afterwards, and 10% back in points towards your next class. Book your date in the Reserve Seat portal and the joining details arrive by email. Note that your agent and Higgsfield subscriptions are separate from the course fee.

    What exactly do I need to pay for beforehand?

    Claude Pro or ChatGPT Pro — whichever you already use, from US$20/mo — and Higgsfield (one month minimum; this is where your generation credits come from). Suno's free tier is enough. Both subscriptions can be cancelled after the month. We'd rather be blunt about this now than have you discover it on the day.

    Will I actually finish with a video?

    Yes. You build your character in Part One, generate a full sequence in Part Two, and in Part Three the agent assembles and scores the cut. You leave with a finished piece — and, more importantly, with the pipeline that made it.

    Do I have to build a virtual influencer?

    Not at all. A consistent character is just as useful for a product advertisement, a brand campaign, a corporate explainer or a short film. Jasmin is the demonstration, not the assignment.

    Is it live, or recorded?

    Always live and always face-to-face — three and a half hours in the room with Dr. Andrew Chew at Paya Lebar Square, deliberately small so Andrew can reach every desk more than once, with follow-up query support afterwards. There is no online version of this class: the pipeline is involved enough that you want someone able to look at your actual screen.
